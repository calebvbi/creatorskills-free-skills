### Why This Works

**The summary sells the episode without spoiling it.** Instead of listing every topic, it focuses on the most compelling narrative arc — the journey from zero to six figures — and uses specific numbers (40,000 readers, 12,000 in one quarter, 2,000 lost) to make the story tangible.

**Timestamps are action-oriented.** Each chapter title describes what the listener will learn, not just the topic. "The referral program breakdown" tells the listener exactly what they'll get if they skip to that section.

**Social captions are tailored to each platform.** The Instagram version is longer and hooks with a story. The Twitter version is a punchy standalone thought. The LinkedIn version speaks to professionals. Same episode, three different angles.

**SEO is baked in naturally.** The primary keyword "grow a newsletter" appears in the summary without feeling forced. Guest name, specific tools, and strategy terms are all searchable phrases that will pull in organic traffic.

### Why This Works

**Outline-based show notes still deliver a complete package.** Even without a transcript, the structured outline gave enough detail to write a compelling summary, extract key themes, and build social captions. Timestamps are clearly marked as estimates so the creator can adjust them after recording.

**The summary focuses on outcomes, not topics.** Instead of listing "5 things about digital products," it highlights the most counterintuitive insights — underpricing is worse than overpricing, the landing page matters more than the product. These are the hooks that convince non-listeners to press play.

**Key quotes are constructed from the outline's core insights.** Since there's no transcript to pull from, the quotes capture the spirit of each point in a shareable, tweet-worthy format that the host can adjust to match their exact phrasing after recording.

## Key Takeaways From Both Examples

1. **Show notes are marketing, not transcription.** The goal is to convince non-listeners to press play and help existing listeners find specific moments. Every section should serve one of those two purposes.

2. **Social captions need platform-specific angles.** The same episode produces different hooks for Instagram (story-driven), Twitter (one punchy thought), and LinkedIn (professional framing). One generic post doesn't work.

3. **Outline input works when transcript isn't available.** The system produces estimated timestamps and builds the best package it can, clearly flagging what the creator needs to fill in after recording.

4. **Specific numbers sell episodes.** "$80K in digital products" is more compelling than "a lot of digital product revenue." "12,000 subscribers in 3 months" beats "a bunch of new subscribers." Every number in the show notes earns its place.
